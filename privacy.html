Privacy Policy

Last updated 28 November 2026. Think First is made by Yunseong Kim, Republic of Korea. Contact: hello@trythinkfirst.com

Think First comes in two editions. Both keep everything on your Mac. The Mac App Store edition doesn't connect to the internet at all; the edition downloaded from this site only checks GitHub for updates.

There is also a separate classroom edition, Think First for Schools, with a Teacher Dashboard. It works differently: if a student joins a class, numbers about their AI use are shared with their teacher. It's described in its own section below.

Mac App Store edition

Think First has no accounts, analytics or ads, and we have no server. What it records stays on your Mac. We can't see it.

What stays on your Mac

Think First keeps one file on your Mac, which holds:

  • the questions it noticed you asking an AI
  • your attempts, and whether you tried first or skipped
  • the AI's answers (up to 6,000 characters each), the gap results and your review cards
  • the title of the AI window, such as a conversation's name, and when you asked
  • your settings and Noodle's name and look

The file is ~/Library/Containers/com.thinkfirst.noodle.appstore/Data/Library/Application Support/think-first/thinkfirst.json. It isn't encrypted, so anyone or any app with access to your Mac user account could read it, and your own backups, such as Time Machine, may copy it.

What the permissions are used for

  • Noticing a question: while an AI app is in front, Think First looks at that app's window a few times a second, and reads its chat box with Apple's on-device text recognition, to notice when you send a message. It doesn't use your keyboard. Nothing it sees is saved except the question you sent.
  • Screen Recording: takes pictures of the AI chat window to see when the answer has finished and, to read your question and the AI's answer from them. Reading is done on your Mac. The pictures are held in memory and are never saved or sent.
  • Front window: checks the name and title of the window in front to tell whether it's an AI chat. It only keeps the title of AI chat windows.

You can turn these off in System Settings > Privacy & Security, though Noodle can't work without them. Pause and quiet hours stop him for a while.

What leaves your Mac

Nothing. This edition of Think First doesn't connect to the internet. Updates come through the Mac App Store, under Apple's own privacy policy. Your questions, attempts, answers and stats never leave your Mac.

AI services

Think First doesn't send anything to AI services for you. What you type into ChatGPT, Claude or any other AI goes to that company under its own terms and privacy policy, just as it would without Think First.

Deleting your data

  • Remove one question from its entry in History.
  • Delete everything with Reset in Settings.
  • Deleting the app doesn't delete your data. To remove it too, delete the think-first folder shown above.

We hold no copy, so we can't recover deleted data, and there's nothing for us to hand over or delete on request.

Children

We don't knowingly collect anything from anyone, including children, because we don't collect your data at all. If you're too young to agree to these terms where you live, a parent or guardian should agree for you.

Changes and questions

If this changes, the new version will appear here in an update, with a new date.

Think First for Schools and the Teacher Dashboard

Think First for Schools is a separate app for classes. Everything works as in the edition above, except that a student can join their teacher's class. Then numbers about how they use AI are shared with that teacher, never their questions, attempts or the AI's answers.

What students share, and only after joining

Nothing is shared until a student joins a class with a code from their teacher and agrees to a screen listing exactly what is shared. After that, sharing only happens while the teacher has started class from the Teacher Dashboard (the app shows “Class is live”). For each question asked during a live class, the app shares:

  • when they asked, and whether they tried first or skipped
  • the subject it guessed, such as Biology
  • which AI it was (for example ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini) and whether it was the app or a website
  • how many words their attempt had, and how many points they got, missed and added (counts only)
  • the name they typed when joining, and when the app was last active

Questions asked at any other time, such as at home, at weekends or in other lessons, are never shared and never leave the Mac, and the database refuses them. It never shares the questions, what the student wrote, the AI's answers, window titles, screenshots, or anything from before they joined. Students get an anonymous sign-in that lives only on their Mac, with no email or password.

What teachers share

Teachers sign in to the Teacher Dashboard with an emailed link or with Google. We keep their email address, the name they choose (shown to students), an optional school name, the names, codes, icons and colours of the classes they create, and when they started and ended each class. If a teacher signs in with Google, Google shares their name, email address and profile picture with us; we use the name and email only to sign them in and suggest their display name.

Where it's stored and who can see it

This data is stored in a database run with Supabase (hosted in Seoul, Republic of Korea), under Supabase's own privacy policy. Database rules ensure that a student can only add and read their own numbers, and a teacher can only see the classes they own and the students in them. We don't sell this data or use it for advertising, and there are no analytics or ads.

Leaving and deleting

  • Students can leave a class at any time from the Class page, which stops sharing straight away, and can choose to delete everything they shared.
  • Teachers can remove a student from a class, which deletes that student's numbers from the class.
  • To have a teacher account or other data deleted, email us at the address above.

Schools and children

Think First for Schools is meant to be used with a school's agreement. Joining a class is always the student's own choice. If a student is too young to agree to this where they live, a parent or guardian should agree for them, and their school may also ask for their consent.
